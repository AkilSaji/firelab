Method

  • FireLab: the engine: Go-Explore archive with frontier and depth selection, dive order, coverage reward, focus walks
  • FireLab (MVP): ablation: the first engine version (depth capped at 20, no frontier term, fixed episodes, no dive order, no coverage reward)
  • Random: uniform random remote keys, same pace and oracles
  • Monkey: Android Monkey's D-pad event mix (nav 60%, majornav 35%, syskeys 5%), same pace and oracles

Every strategy runs the same games with the same wall-clock budget, the same remote keys and the same passive checks (crash, JS exception, black or frozen screen, stopped render loop); only FireLab walks the D-pad focus. One seed per run; runs are interleaved across strategies so machine load is shared. Games run in headless Chrome at the Fire TV WebView's 960×540 viewport, with keys delivered as FireLab's TV wrapper delivers them.

Ground truth. Each seeded bug records in the page when it fires, whoever drives the game. Triggered counts those; reported also needs a finding of the matching kind from the tester. JS coverage is V8 precise block coverage of the game's own scripts, which no strategy can inflate. FireLab's own state count and depth are shown for context only.

Statistics. Medians with inter-quartile ranges; two-sided Mann-Whitney U with Vargha-Delaney Â12 effect sizes for continuous metrics; Fisher's exact test per seeded bug, following Arcuri and Briand (ICSE 2011) and Klees et al. (CCS 2018). With 5 runs per cell, treat p-values as indicative; Klees et al. recommend 30 runs, and recent fuzzing practice accepts 10 or more.

For reference (published results on Android apps, a different setting, not comparable one to one): on Themis's 52 real crash bugs (Su et al., FSE 2021; 6 h × 5 runs) Ape found 24, Monkey 22, ComboDroid 21, Stoat 19, Humanoid 18, TimeMachine 15 and Q-testing 10, and 18 bugs were found by no tool, 12 of them because they need more than 5 page transitions. On AndroTest (Choudhary et al., ASE 2015; 68 apps, 1 h × 10 runs) Monkey had the best coverage and every tool plateaued within 5-10 minutes. No TV or D-pad benchmark has been published; Deep Quest is FireLab's own depth benchmark, and Firefly Lake's bugs mirror bugs found in a shipped TV game.
